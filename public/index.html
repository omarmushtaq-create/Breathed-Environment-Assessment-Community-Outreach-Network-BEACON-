COMMUNITY AIR QUALITY PROJECT

Air quality data
for the places
maps forget

BEACON builds low-cost, solar-powered air quality sensors and deploys them in underfunded neighborhoods that regulatory monitoring stations don't reach — then publishes every reading live, for free, so residents can see the air they're actually breathing on their own street.

Why this exists

The nearest air monitor
might be miles away

Most cities rely on a small handful of regulatory-grade air quality stations, often spaced miles apart. That works fine for a broad regional picture — but it tells you almost nothing about the air on your specific block, especially if you live near a highway, an industrial corridor, or anywhere else official monitoring just doesn't reach.

That gap falls hardest on lower-income neighborhoods, which are frequently closer to pollution sources and furthest from monitoring infrastructure. Without local data, it's hard to even prove there's a problem — let alone advocate for fixing it.

BEACON is one person's attempt to close that gap: cheap, replicable hardware, deployed block by block, with the data made fully public rather than locked behind an agency's dashboard.

How it works

From sunlight to
a live reading

01

Solar + battery

A small solar panel keeps a rechargeable battery topped up, so each unit runs fully independent of the power grid, day or night.

02

Sense

A particulate sensor and a temperature/humidity sensor take a fresh reading on a short cycle, right at street level.

03

Upload

Each reading is sent over WiFi straight into a public database — no app, no login required to view it.

04

Publish

The live dashboard below updates automatically, so anyone can check current and historical air quality for a given sensor.
